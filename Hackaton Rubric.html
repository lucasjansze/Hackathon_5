Hackathon Assessment Rubric

Purpose of this rubric

This rubric is used to assess each hackathon separately. It focuses on whether teams have identified a relevant problem, built and demonstrated a meaningful prototype, and can explain and justify the choices they made.

The assessment consists of two parts:

  1. GitHub portfolio submission
    Every week, each team submits the slides and demo materials to GitHub. The submitted material must make sense on its own without additional explanation. Everything that is required according to the rubric should be documented on GitHub. You can submit your github repo link here (please login with your HHS account)

  2. Live presentation and demo (only applied if your team is selected to present) 
    For the selected teams, the pitch, live demo, and answers to questions are assessed alongside the portfolio submission. This provides the teachers an opportunity to check whether the students understand their own work. 

Being selected to present does not change the criteria, scoring, or maximum grade. A team that is not selected is assessed only on the portfolio submission. A team that does not submit a portfolio receives no grade, regardless of the live presentation

How grading works

The rubric has two stages:

  1. First, we assess the three knockout criteria. These are pass/fail requirements. Students must meet all three knockout criteria to receive a grade. If even one criterion is not met, the hackathon receives no grade and the remaining criteria are not assessed. 
  2. If all knockout criteria are met, the sixed scored criteria will be assessed. Together, these add up to a maximum of 10 points.

Points are awarded only for work that is shown and can be explained by the team. You are graded on what you show and defend, not on what you intended. An idea or intended feature does not count if it is not demonstrated, documented in the slides or portfolio, or explained convincingly when asked. 

Knockout criteria 

Criterion Not met (o) — no grade Met (v)
K1 — AI tool
The required AI tool is used meaningfully in the prototype. 		 The required tool is not used, has been replaced by another tool, or is only mentioned in the pitch. The prototype would still work in the same way without it.  The required tool is integrated into the prototype and performs a clear, necessary step. The team can show what it does and explain why the prototype would not work as intended without it. 
K2 — SDG relevance
The solution addresses a concrete issue within the assigned SDG.		 The assigned SDG is not addressed, or it appears only as a logo or a sentence with no effect on what the solution actually does. The team identifies a specific issue within the assigned SDG, explains how the solution addresses it, and identifies who would benefit if the solution works. 
K3 — Scope
The delivered solution provides meaningful value for its intended users. 		 The expected value for users is limited or unclear. The delivered work is mainly a basic technical exercise, with no realistic use case implemented.  The prototype addresses a real user problem and delivers meaningful, non-trivial functionality. It goes beyond a basic proof of concept or isolated technical experiment. 

Scored criteria — 10 points in total

Criterion Not met Partly met Fully met
1. Problem definition
The problem is specific, clearly placed in context, and supported by evidence. 		 0 points
There is no recognisable problem, or the problem is too broad to guide a solution.		 1 point
 The identified problem is real and understandable, but remains general. The setting, scale, urgency, or evidence of the problem is missing.		 2 points
The problem is clearly defined: what is going wrong, for whom, where, and when. The team indicates how significant it is and supports this with a source, data point, observation, or concrete example. 
2. User group
The intended users are named and characterised, including who is left out		 0 points
No user group is identified, or the target group is described only in broad terms. 		 1 point
A broad group is named, but the team provides little information about their situation, needs, or limitations. 		 2 points
The intended users are specific and well described. The team explains their situation, needs, and relevant conditions of use. The team also states who is not the intended user. 
3. Solution description
What you built is described concretely enough for someone else to rebuild it		 0 points
It is unclear what the team has built or how the proposed solution is supposed to work. 		 1 point
The solution is described only in general terms. A reader cannot follow the flow from input to output, or cannot see what the required AI tool contributes. 

2 points
The solution is described clearly and concretely. The team shows what users provide as input, what the system does step by step, what output it produces, and exactly where and how the required AI tool is used. 
4. Problem–solution fit
The solution follows logically from the problem and fits the user group		 0 points
The solution does not address the stated problem, or it appears that a tool was selected first and a problem was added afterwards. 		 0.5 point
The solution is related to the problem, but it is not a good fit in scale, purpose, or practical use. 		 1 point
The solution is a convincing response to the identified problem. The team can explain why this approach fits these users and why a simpler alternative would not achieve the same result.
5. Working prototype
The prototype can be demonstrated from input to output		 0 points
There is no working prototype. The team shows only an idea, a description, wireframes, or a static mock-up. 		 0.5 point
The prototype works only partly. It may fail with normal input, require manual intervention during the flow, or demonstrate only one part of the intended functionality. 		 1 point
The prototype works end to end. The intended input can be entered or provided, the system completes the intended process without manual patching, and it produces the intended output. 
6. Ethical reasoning
Ethical risks specific to this prototype are identified and acted on		 0 points
There is no ethical reflection, or only a generic line about AI and bias that could be pasted onto any project.		 1 point
The team identifies one or more relevant risks, but does not explain the consequences for users or how the risk should be addressed. 		 2 points
The team identifies ethical risks specific to the prototype, explains the likely consequences for the intended users, and states what has been done or would be done next to reduce these risks.
